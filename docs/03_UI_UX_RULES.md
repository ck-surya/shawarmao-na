# UI/UX Rules — Anti-Vibe-Coding Guide

## Design goal
A real restaurant ordering experience designed by a human product/brand designer. Prioritize food photography, typography, spacing, hierarchy, menu usability and subtle interaction design.

## Brand feel
Premium but approachable; late-night food destination; Lebanese/Middle-Eastern character; strong food photography; editorial restaurant feel; fast and practical ordering.

## Avoid list
1. Harsh gradients
2. Generic SaaS pricing tiers
3. Pure white page aesthetic
4. Rainbow colors
5. Excessive drop shadows
6. Generic feature-card rows
7. Emojis as UI icons
8. Glassmorphism/liquid glass
9. Em-dash-heavy AI copy
10. Inter/Geist/Space Grotesk as defining brand typography
11. Colored left stripes
12. Fake testimonials
13. Generic bento grids
14. Fake terminal/code windows
15. “It’s not X, it’s Y” marketing copy
16. Generic checkmark marketing bullets
17. Fake product demos
18. Purple/black AI aesthetic
19. Neon colors
20. Decorative radial glowing orbs
21. Decorative skeleton loaders
22. Dot grids
23. Sparkle icons
24. Excessive animated arrows
25. Over-animation
26. Missing legal pages
27. Generic startup feature walls
28. Generic pastel template appearance

## Functional exceptions
Lucide icons are fine when functional. Skeleton loaders are fine for real loading. Hover animation is fine when subtle and useful. Animated arrows are fine sparingly. Moderate corner radius is fine.

## Palette
Warm off-white, charcoal, deep red, restrained warm orange, muted beige and natural food colors. Avoid neon, rainbow, purple/black AI palette and excessive gradients.

## Typography
Use an expressive food/editorial display typeface for headings with a readable sans-serif for body/UI. Do not make it resemble a developer portfolio or SaaS dashboard.

## Icons
Use functional icons such as search, shopping bag/cart, map pin, phone, clock, plus/minus, chevron and arrow. Do not place icons beside every sentence.

## Components
Sticky header; category rail; popular items; menu cards; add/quantity stepper; cart drawer/bottom sheet; item customization sheet; feedback toast/minicart; checkout; confirmation; tracking timeline; admin order board; useful loading/error/empty states.

## Motion
150–250ms microinteractions; 250–400ms sheets/panels. Use opacity/transform/subtle scale. Respect prefers-reduced-motion. Avoid bouncing, particles, constant movement and animation on every card.

## Mobile
Design first for 375–430px. One-hand usability. Menu and cart remain easy to access while scrolling.

## Photography
Use real restaurant/product photography whenever possible. Do not substitute generic stock imagery if actual restaurant images are available.

## Copy
Short, natural, restaurant-specific and useful. No corporate filler, fake claims, empty superlatives or AI slogans.

## UX priority
Food discovery → menu comprehension → add to cart → checkout → confirmation → tracking → decorative polish.
